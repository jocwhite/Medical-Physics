Oral Exam Answer

What is TPS commissioning?

TPS commissioning is the process of configuring, modeling, validating, and clinically verifying a treatment planning system using measured machine data before patient treatments are performed.

Goal: Make sure the TPS accurately models beam output, PDDs, profiles, heterogeneity effects, MLC behavior, and dose delivery under realistic clinical conditions.
Workflow

Typical TPS Commissioning Workflow

  • Acceptance testing
  • Beam data acquisition and processing
  • Import measured data into the TPS
  • Generate and tune the beam model
  • Validate open fields, heterogeneity, small fields, IMRT, and VMAT
  • Complete end-to-end testing and clinical release
Dose Algorithms

Algorithm Comparison

Algorithm Core Idea Strength Weakness / Oral Trap
Pencil Beam Summes narrow beamlets with simple density scaling. Very fast. Poor in lung, interfaces, small fields, and lateral electron disequilibrium.
Collapsed Cone Convolution/superposition with energy transported along discrete cones. Good balance of speed and accuracy. Still approximate transport.
AAA Anisotropic convolution-superposition with primary, extra-focal, and electron contamination sources. Fast and clinically useful. Can struggle in very small lung fields and severe disequilibrium.
Acuros XB Deterministically solves the Linear Boltzmann Transport Equation. Excellent heterogeneity handling. Slower than simpler algorithms.
Monte Carlo Statistically simulates individual particle histories. Gold-standard transport physics. Statistical noise and longer computation time.
High-Yield Concept

Dose-to-Water vs Dose-to-Medium

Dose-to-medium is energy deposited in the actual physical medium. Dose-to-water converts that result into an equivalent water dose using stopping-power ratios.

Differences matter most in bone, high-Z materials, and heterogeneous interfaces.
Beam Modeling

Beam Model Parameters

  • Primary source size and energy spectrum
  • Flattening filter or FFF modeling
  • Extra-focal radiation and electron contamination
  • MLC transmission, DLG, and tongue-and-groove effects
  • Jaw geometry and penumbra modeling
Validation

Final High-Yield Checklist

You should be able to explain:

  • How PB, CCC, AAA, Acuros, and Monte Carlo work
  • How heterogeneity is handled and why lung is difficult
  • Dose-to-water vs dose-to-medium
  • How beam models are created and tuned
  • What DLG represents clinically
  • How to troubleshoot beam-model discrepancies
  • Why validation must be independent from commissioning data
  • IMRT vs VMAT commissioning concerns
Oral-board sentence: A TPS is only as accurate as its beam model, commissioning measurements, and validation methodology.

Practice Questions

Collapsible oral-board questions for fast recall.

How is Acuros different from Monte Carlo?

Answer: Monte Carlo statistically simulates individual particle histories using random sampling. Acuros deterministically solves transport equations on a spatial grid.

Why do pencil beam algorithms fail in lung?

Answer: They mainly use radiological-depth scaling and do not accurately model lateral electron transport. In lung, longer electron ranges can produce disequilibrium that pencil beam algorithms cannot handle well.

How do you generate a CT-density table?

Answer: Scan a CT-density phantom with known inserts, measure HU values, and map those HU values to electron or mass density for TPS dose calculation. Different protocols or kVp settings may need separate curves.
